# Historical population grids for a dated map

Date: 2026-09-25

Notes from primary sources only: the European Commission Joint Research Centre GHSL pages, the GHSL Data Package 2023, the JRC open-data FTP, WorldPop’s hub, methods, FAQ, and API pages, NASA SEDAC and the GPWv4 Revision 11 documentation, Utrecht University’s HYDE datasets page and the HYDE 3.2 data paper, the UN Population Division’s World Population Prospects 2024 page, Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s LandScan data descriptor, Eurostat GISCO, national statistical offices, and the producer pages for the other grids named below. Download sizes are the figures those servers published when the pages were read on this date.

Project Zomboid internals are out of scope. The product constraint used here is the one already in the generator: zombie counts follow residential floor area, and a useful extra layer has to vary inside a town. A 100 m cell can scale that footprint model. A 5–10 km cell is a regional dial. Country totals cannot place people on a street.

## 1. GHS-POP R2023A

The current short-range population grid on the GHSL datasets page is GHS-POP R2023A, resident population, epochs 1975–2030, resolutions 100 m, 1 km, 3 arc-seconds, and 30 arc-seconds, in World Mollweide and WGS84. The citation on that page is Schiavina, Freire, Carioli, and MacManus (2023), DOI [10.2905/2FF68A52-5B5B-4A22-8F40-C41DA8332CFE](https://doi.org/10.2905/2FF68A52-5B5B-4A22-8F40-C41DA8332CFE), PID [http://data.europa.eu/89h/2ff68a52-5b5b-4a22-8f40-c41da8332cfe](http://data.europa.eu/89h/2ff68a52-5b5b-4a22-8f40-c41da8332cfe). ([GHSL datasets](https://ghsl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/datasets.php))

The JRC open-data FTP lists one directory per epoch from 1975 through 2030 at a five-year step: 1975, 1980, 1985, 1990, 1995, 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, 2025, 2030. Each epoch has a 100 m Mollweide layer, a 1 km Mollweide layer, a 3 arc-second WGS84 layer, and a 30 arc-second WGS84 layer. ([GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/))

The GHSL Data Package 2023, published by the Publications Office of the European Union (JRC133256), describes the grid as the number of people per cell. Residential population estimates at a five-year interval from 1975 to 2030 are taken from the census polygons harmonized by CIESIN for GPWv4.11 and disaggregated onto grid cells using the distribution, classification, and volume of built-up mapped in the GHSL layers for the matching epoch. The target layer is total built-up volume minus non-residential built-up volume at each five-year step. Polygon totals are rescaled to the UN World Urbanization Prospects 2018 city series and to the UN World Population Prospects 2022 country series. The 100 m grids are produced in World Mollweide (ESRI:54009) and aggregated to 1 km; those two grids are then warped to WGS84 at 3 arc-seconds and 30 arc-seconds. Values are float64 population counts, with NoData −200. Files are GeoTIFF, as one global layer with pyramids or as tiles. ([GHSL Data Package 2023](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC133256/JRC133256_01.pdf))

The same report’s summary table of the 1 km Mollweide grid, adjusted to UN WPP 2022, gives world totals of 4,069,437,259 in 1975, 5,316,175,909 in 1990, 5,743,219,510 in 1995, 6,148,899,024 in 2000, 7,840,952,947 in 2020, and 8,546,141,407 in 2030. ([GHSL Data Package 2023](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC133256/JRC133256_01.pdf))

The release note treats 1975–2020 as the past, at a five-year interval, and 2025 and 2030 as the future years in the same package. A later product, GHS-WUP-POP R2025A, continues resident population from 1975 to 2100 at a five-year interval, at 1 km and 30 arc-seconds. That long-range grid is coarser than the 100 m R2023A layers. ([P2023 release](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/p2023Release.php), [GHSL datasets](https://ghsl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/datasets.php))

1993 sits between the 1990 and 1995 epochs. The FTP directory names contain no 1993 layer.

The FTP `copyright.txt` shipped with the R2023A population grids says any copyright and sui generis right on the dataset is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. Reuse is allowed when appropriate credit is given and any changes are indicated. ([copyright.txt](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/copyright.txt)) The GHSL citation page repeats CC BY 4.0, including commercial use, and says appropriate credit means the peer-reviewed release paper plus the product citation on the download page. ([How to cite](http://ghsl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/GHSLhowToCite.php))

Access on the JRC side is the download wizard and the open-data FTP, by global file or by tile. The 2020 100 m Mollweide global zip is `GHS_POP_E2020_GLOBE_R2023A_54009_100_V1_0.zip`, 4.7 GB. The matching `tiles/` index lists per-tile zips from about 1.8 MB to 20 MB; one file in that listing, `…_R8_C20.zip`, is 20 MB. ([2020 100 m](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/GHS_POP_E2020_GLOBE_R2023A_54009_100/V1-0/), [tiles](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/GHS_POP_E2020_GLOBE_R2023A_54009_100/V1-0/tiles/)) No STAC or bounding-box REST endpoint is described on the GHSL download pages read for this note. The JRC data-catalogue URL for the dataset DOI returned HTTP 403.

## 2. WorldPop

The WorldPop hub still publishes the Global High Resolution Population Denominators project as unconstrained country files for each year 2000–2020 at 3 arc-seconds, described as approximately 100 m at the equator. Units are people per pixel, geographic WGS84, Random Forest dasymetric redistribution of subnational census figures. GeoTIFF. A parallel set is adjusted to United Nations national totals (UN 2019). The same hub lists unconstrained 1 km country files and unconstrained global 1 km mosaics for 2000–2020, and constrained country files at 100 m for 2020, with and without the UN adjustment. ([Global 2000–2020](https://hub.worldpop.org/Global1_2000-2020), [Unconstrained 100 m](https://hub.worldpop.org/geodata/listing?id=29), [Constrained 2020](https://hub.worldpop.org/geodata/listing?id=78))

The data server lists `usa_ppp_2020.tif` at 3.7 GB and `usa_ppp_2020_UNadj.tif` at 3.7 GB. ([USA 2020](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/USA/))

The FAQ states the licence as Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International: use, copy, distribute, transmit, and adapt, commercially and otherwise, with clear attribution. The same page says one version of the layers is adjusted to census totals and another to UN national estimates. It also says the data are not intended for uses that discriminate against, exploit, surveil, or otherwise harm individuals, and that users are responsible for their own analyses. The file `https://www.worldpop.org/data/licence.txt`, linked from the REST metadata, returned HTTP 404 on this date; the FAQ text is the licence statement that loaded. ([FAQ](https://www.worldpop.org/faq/))

A newer beta, documented by WorldPop as public release R2024B, is an open set of 3 arc-second grids for 242 countries, age- and sex-structured, annual for 2015–2030, with national totals aligned to World Population Prospects 2024. The release statement says reviews are still under way and flags unrealistic zero estimates in northern Pakistan. Subnational counts come from the circa-2010 and circa-2020 census rounds, then interpolated and projected. Settlement inputs include GHS-BUILT-S and GHS-BUILT-V at 2015, 2020, 2025, and 2030, plus Google and Microsoft building footprints. ([R2024B release statement](https://data.worldpop.org/repo/prj/Global_2015_2030/R2024B/doc/Global2_Release_Statement_R2024B_v1.pdf))

The methods page defines the two top-down modes for that 2015–2030 work. Unconstrained estimation writes a population number for every 100 m land cell. Constrained estimation uses Maxar/Ecopia, Google, and Microsoft footprints together with GHSL and World Settlement Footprint inside a built-settlement growth model, then disaggregates only onto cells mapped as residential buildings or built settlement, with building class from GHSL and OpenStreetMap. ([Constrained and unconstrained](https://www.worldpop.org/methods/top_down_constrained_vs_unconstrained/))

The documented stats API is `https://api.worldpop.org/v1/services/stats` with dataset `wpgppop` or `wpgpas`, a year from 2000 to 2020, and a GeoJSON polygon. `wpgppop` is described as the global per-country 100 m counts for each year 2000–2020. The response field is `total_population` for that polygon, or an age-sex pyramid for `wpgpas`. The default is asynchronous; `runasync=false` waits up to 30 seconds. The rate-limit page allows 1,000 calls per day without an API key. A separate REST index at `https://www.worldpop.org/rest/data` returns metadata and an FTP path for a country-year GeoTIFF. ([Advanced API](https://www.worldpop.org/sdi/advancedapi/), [Rate limits](https://www.worldpop.org/sdi/api_rate_limits/), [API basics](https://www.worldpop.org/sdi/introapi/))

The stats call returns one total for the polygon. It does not return the 100 m cells inside the town. The cell grid is the country GeoTIFF.

2000 is the first year of the global 100 m annual country files. 1993 is earlier than that series.

## 3. GPWv4.11

GPWv4 Revision 11 population count is estimates of persons per pixel, consistent with national censuses and population registers, for 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2020. About 13.5 million national and sub-national units are allocated to 30 arc-second cells, about 1 km at the equator, then aggregated to 2.5 arc-minutes, 15 arc-minutes, 30 arc-minutes, and 1 degree. Density grids are the count grid divided by the land-area grid. DOI for the count grid: [10.7927/H4JW8BX5](https://doi.org/10.7927/H4JW8BX5). ([Count metadata](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/metadata/gpw-v4-population-count-rev11.html), [What’s new](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/data/collection/gpw-v4/whatsnew))

The Revision 11 documentation says the inputs are the 2010 round of censuses, taken between 2005 and 2014, extrapolated to those five target years. A second set is adjusted to the UN World Population Prospects 2015 Revision. Both sets are spread by uniform areal weighting: each administrative unit’s count is shared across the cells in proportion to area. The documentation calls this minimally modelled. Global rasters are GeoTIFF, ASCII, and netCDF-4, in WGS84. ASCII and GeoTIFF exist at 30 arc-seconds and at the four coarser resolutions. netCDF-4 exists at every resolution except 30 arc-seconds, and only as all years combined. The 30 arc-second ASCII grids are split into eight tiles because of file size. The download page describes each item as a global zip. It does not describe a bounding-box request. The NASA Earthdata catalog lists WMS among the formats for the count collection. A sentence requiring an Earthdata login was not on the download page text that loaded. ([GPWv4 documentation, Revision 11](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/docs/gpw-v4/gpw-v4-documentation-rev11.pdf), [Download](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/data/set/gpw-v4-population-count-rev11/data-download), [Earthdata catalog](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/sedac-ciesin-sedac-gpwv4-popcount-r11-4.11))

The count metadata states CC BY 4.0: use, copy, distribute, transmit, and adapt, commercially and otherwise, with attribution. ([Count metadata](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/metadata/gpw-v4-population-count-rev11.html))

The five target years begin in 2000. 1993 is earlier than the revision.

SEDAC’s older GRUMPv1 population density grid, which the same centre now points users away from in favour of GPWv4, estimates people per square kilometre for 1990, 1995, and 2000 at 30 arc-seconds, again by proportional allocation, with urban extents informed by nighttime lights. The collection page says the grids are also available as continental, regional, and national subsets, and that WMS is offered. The download page says GPWv4 supersedes GRUMPv1 and GPWv3. ([GRUMP collection](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/data/collection/grump-v1), [GRUMP density](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/data/set/grump-v1-population-density))

GRUMPv1 therefore has a 1990 layer and a 1995 layer at about 1 km. It has no 1993 layer. GPWv4, the product SEDAC tells users to take instead, starts at 2000.

## 4. HYDE

The PBL Netherlands page for HYDE returned “Access denied” on this date. The current description is the datasets page of the Copernicus Land Change Lab at Utrecht University, which PBL’s IMAGE data page points to for downloads.

That page lists HYDE 3.5 (2025) as the latest entry: 5 arc-minutes, about 85 km² at the equator, from 10 000 BCE to 2025 CE. HYDE 3.4 (2024) runs to 2024, HYDE 3.3 (2023) to 2023, and HYDE 3.2 (2017) to 2020, all at the same 5 arc-minute grid. ([HYDE datasets](https://landuse.sites.uu.nl/datasets/))

The HYDE portal, which says it is showing HYDE 3.3, describes global maps at 5 arc-minutes, about 9.21 × 9.21 km, and says the full data, including NetCDF and zipped indicator files, can be downloaded. ([HYDE portal](https://hyde-portal.geo.uu.nl/))

The HYDE 3.2 paper, written by the dataset authors at PBL and published in Earth System Science Data, says population is mapped as total, urban, and rural population, population density, and built-up area, from 10 000 BCE to 2015 CE. From 1950 the population backbone is the UN World Population Prospects 2008 Revision; earlier centuries use historical compilations allocated to present-day provinces. Present-day spatial pattern in that version uses LandScan 2014 weights for 2012. The Utrecht dataset record for HYDE 3.2.1 says the files are ESRI ASCII grids at 5 arc-minutes. ([HYDE 3.2](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-9-927-2017), [Yoda record](https://doi.org/10.24416/uu01-xutqn5))

The year step of HYDE 3.5 is not stated on the datasets page. The 3.2 paper’s summary table reports population at selected years (5000 BCE, 1 CE, 500, 1000, 1500, 1700, 1850, 2015) and does not, in the text that loaded, state whether the files are annual, decadal, or centennial. A redistribution licence was not on the datasets page that loaded.

At about 9 km, a HYDE cell covers a whole town. It can scale a region across centuries. It cannot vary density from one neighbourhood to the next.

## 5. UN World Population Prospects 2024

The 2024 Revision is the twenty-eighth edition from the Population Division of the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs. It gives population estimates from 1950 to the present for 237 countries or areas, drawing on 1,910 national censuses from 1950 to 2023, vital registration, and 3,189 surveys, plus projections to 2100. Results are Excel files for development groups, income groups, geographic regions, SDG regions, subregions, and countries or areas, for dates inside 1950–2100. A Data Portal exposes a subset of indicators and an open API. Bulk use is CSV. Since the 2022 revision, estimates and projections are in one-year steps of age and time. The estimates run 1950–2023 and the projections 2024–2100. ([World Population Prospects](https://population.un.org/wpp/))

1993 is inside that annual country series. The geography of a record is a country, a region, or a named group of countries. The page does not publish a grid, a city, or a census tract. A game that has only this series can change the national headcount for the United States between 1993 and 2020. It cannot change how those people sit inside Knox County, or inside one neighbourhood of it.

A redistribution licence was not on the WPP page that loaded.

## 6. LandScan Global

The ORNL data descriptor in Scientific Data, “LandScan Global 30 Arcsecond Annual Global Gridded Population Datasets from 2000 to 2022”, says Oak Ridge National Laboratory produces an annual 30 arc-second grid of ambient population. Ambient means an unwarned 24-hour average: where people might be across the day, including activity away from the residence where a census usually counts them. Census totals are disaggregated inside administrative boundaries with a multivariable dasymetric model. Buildings are not split into residential and commercial, because that split would pull the surface back toward a nighttime residential map. The authors say cell-level change between years should be avoided, because inputs and methods change through the series. ([Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04817-z))

The same paper’s data record says the 2000–2022 rasters are freely available at figshare ([10.6084/m9.figshare.28439699](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28439699)) and at [landscan.ornl.gov](https://landscan.ornl.gov/). Each raster is WGS84, 21,600 rows by 43,200 columns, 933,120,000 cells of 30 arc-seconds, and stores a population count per cell. The portal also draws a polygon and returns a count and a density for that polygon. ([Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04817-z))

The series in that paper starts in 2000. 1993 is earlier. LandScan HD, for selected countries, and LandScan USA are 3 arc-second products; LandScan USA keeps daytime and nighttime separate, and the global grid averages them where that input exists. ([Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04817-z))

When the DOI [10.48690/1532445](https://doi.org/10.48690/1532445) was opened it resolved to the LandScan viewer, which rendered year buttons through 2026. The viewer is a scripted page; the data descriptor that loaded as text stops at 2022. A licence paragraph for redistributing the raster, or for baking a sampled count into a published mod, was not in the Data Records section. The portal’s own terms page did not return as text (the about URL timed out; the homepage is the map shell).

## 8. National census geography

These products show what an official census looks like inside one country. They do not combine into one worldwide time series.

Japan’s Statistics Bureau compiles grid-square statistics from the 2020 Population Census, taken at 00:00 on 1 October 2020. The standard grid, fixed by administrative order in 1973, is about 1 km, 500 m, and 250 m on a side, on JGD 2000. Basic unit blocks are allocated into those squares. Small counts are summed into neighbouring squares before release. The bureau says grid-square statistics have been compiled since the 1965 census, and that the 1965 release was tentative and limited to areas such as metropolitan regions. Download is through e-Stat. ([2020 outline (PDF)](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/mesh/pdf/r2outline.pdf), [2020 mesh page](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/mesh/r2_w.html))

The finest Japan mesh in that outline is 250 m. That is a census grid, for one country, at census years, and it stops above a building footprint.

For England and Wales, ONS Output Areas are the lowest census geography, first built after the 2001 Census. A 2011 Output Area that still held 40–250 households and 100–625 usual residents was kept for 2021; otherwise areas were split or merged to stay inside those thresholds. Some areas, such as a large prison, can exceed 625 residents. ([Census 2021 geographies](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/ukgeographies/censusgeographies/census2021geographies))

An Output Area is a polygon drawn around a small population, on the order of a few streets, at a census date. It is not a 100 m raster, and the published thresholds are for 2001, 2011, and 2021.

IPUMS NHGIS, at the University of Minnesota, distributes summary statistics and boundaries from US censuses from 1790 to the present. County and state tables run from 1790. Census tract tables run from 1910, and the NHGIS coverage note says tracts covered eight cities in 1910 and the whole United States only from 1990. Tables for the original census summary levels, down to census blocks, run from 1970. Time-series tables cover basic 100-percent counts from the 1970–2020 censuses. Annual census-tract population estimates by age, sex, and race are listed for 2000–2019. ([Data availability](https://www.nhgis.org/data-availability), [About NHGIS](https://www.nhgis.org/about-ipums-nhgis))

A US census block can be a single city block, which is the scale at which a 1990 or 2000 count can differ inside a town. NHGIS is the United States, at census years. July 1993 falls between the 1990 and 2000 censuses. The about page describes free online access; a redistribution licence for shipping block counts inside a public game mod was not in the text that loaded.

INSEE’s 2021 population census grid for France is 1 km squares. The 200 m carreaux on the same institute’s site are Filosofi fiscal and social-income data, with a confidentiality threshold of 11 tax households, and an imputed value when the threshold fails. That 200 m layer is a different source from the census population grid. ([Census 2021 carreaux](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/8272002), [Filosofi 200 m](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/7655475))

Australia’s ABS defines Mesh Blocks as the smallest ASGS areas, designed where possible to hold 30–60 dwellings. The 2021 census mesh-block file that the ABS publishes at that level is total usual residents and total dwellings: 368,286 Mesh Blocks covering Australia. Finer census characteristics are released for larger areas. ([Mesh Blocks](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/standards/australian-statistical-geography-standard-asgs/edition-3-july-2021-june-2026/main-structure-and-greater-capital-city-statistical-areas/mesh-blocks), [2021 counts](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/guide-census-data/mesh-block-counts/latest-release))

## 12. Answers

The worldwide residential series at about 100 m for 1975–2020 is GHS-POP R2023A. The year step is five years: 1975, 1980, 1985, 1990, 1995, 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, and the same grid continues to projected epochs 2025 and 2030. Cells are residential population, weighted by residential built-up volume. ([GHSL datasets](https://ghsl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/datasets.php), [GHSL Data Package 2023](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC133256/JRC133256_01.pdf))

No worldwide product opened here has a layer dated 1993. GHS-POP brackets 1993 with 1990 and 1995 at 100 m. GRUMPv1 brackets it with 1990 and 1995 at 30 arc-seconds, and SEDAC presents that collection as superseded by GPWv4, whose first year is 2000. UN WPP has a 1993 national total.

The annual worldwide 100 m counts from 2000 are the WorldPop unconstrained country GeoTIFFs, each year 2000–2020, 3 arc-seconds. The global mosaic of that project is 1 km. Constrained 100 m files in that same project are the 2020 country set. The beta R2024B grids are annual at about 100 m from 2015 to 2030, unconstrained and constrained. ([Global 2000–2020](https://hub.worldpop.org/Global1_2000-2020), [R2024B release statement](https://data.worldpop.org/repo/prj/Global_2015_2030/R2024B/doc/Global2_Release_Statement_R2024B_v1.pdf))

Before 1975, the worldwide gridded population product on the Utrecht page is HYDE, at 5 arc-minutes back to 10 000 BCE. UN WPP supplies annual country totals from 1950. GHS-POP starts at 1975.

What the pages do not contain is a worldwide, building-scale, year-by-year census. Japan’s published census mesh stops at 250 m. England and Wales stop at Output Areas of 100–625 usual residents. The US census reaches blocks, from 1970 in NHGIS, inside one country, at census years. INSEE’s census grid is 1 km. None of those offices publish the other countries, and none of the global grids is a census microdata file.

## 14. Europe, Brazil, the United States, Canada, Mexico, Australia, China, Japan, and Russia

That set is inside the two global products already described. GHS-POP R2023A is one globe grid, so each of those countries is on the same 100 m epochs from 1975 to 2030. The census totals under that grid are GPWv4.11. Appendix E of the Revision 11 documentation records a final census or a population register for each of them from the 2010 round: United States 2010 census, Canada 2011 census, Mexico 2010 census, Brazil 2010 census, Australia 2011 census, China 2010 census, Japan 2010 census, Russian Federation 2010 census, United Kingdom 2011 census, France 2009 census, Germany 2011 population register, Italy 2011 census. Hong Kong and Macao are separate rows, both 2011 censuses. ([GPWv4 documentation, Revision 11](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/docs/gpw-v4/gpw-v4-documentation-rev11.pdf))

Appendix F of the same document records the administrative level of the population input. In that table, 0 is the coarsest row, a whole territory. Higher numbers are smaller units, and two numbers mean two levels were merged. For this set the population levels are: United States 5, Brazil 5, Australia 5, Canada 5, China 4, Mexico 3, France 4 and 5, Germany 3, Italy 4, United Kingdom 3, 4, and 6, Japan 2, Russian Federation 2 and 3. Finland, the Netherlands, and Norway are at 2. The document does not convert those levels into kilometres. It tells a user to read the mean administrative unit area layer, and to treat a study area smaller than that mean unit as a lower-confidence total, because GPWv4 spreads each unit evenly by area. GHS-POP then places the same unit totals onto residential built-up volume, so a 100 m cell still varies inside a coarse unit, following the buildings of that epoch. ([GPWv4 documentation, Revision 11](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/docs/gpw-v4/gpw-v4-documentation-rev11.pdf), [GHSL Data Package 2023](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC133256/JRC133256_01.pdf))

WorldPop’s 2020 country index has a directory for each of them, including `AUS`, `BRA`, `CAN`, `CHN`, `DEU`, `FRA`, `GBR`, `JPN`, `MEX`, `RUS`, and `USA`, plus the other European codes on that listing. The 2020 unconstrained GeoTIFF sizes on that server: Russia 16 GB, China 4.6 GB, Brazil 4.1 GB, Australia 3.7 GB, Canada 3.6 GB, Mexico 1.0 GB, France 424 MB, Germany 293 MB, Japan 292 MB, United Kingdom 231 MB. The United States file is 3.7 GB, as noted above. A town in any of these countries is still one GHS tile, a few megabytes to about 20 MB in the 2020 100 m tile index. ([2020 index](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/), [RUS](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/RUS/), [CHN](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/CHN/), [BRA](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/BRA/), [AUS](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/AUS/), [CAN](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/CAN/), [MEX](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/MEX/), [FRA](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/FRA/), [DEU](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/DEU/), [JPN](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/JPN/), [GBR](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/GBR/), [USA](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/USA/))

Eurostat’s 2021 1 km grid covers the European Union and Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland. The Statistics Explained article says Iceland is absent, and that Greece and France used a hybrid of aggregation and disaggregation while the other listed members aggregated geocoded counts. The United Kingdom and Russia are outside that grid. Eurostat is an official 1 km check inside the EU and those three EFTA countries. The layer that also covers the United Kingdom, Russia, Brazil, the Americas, Australia, China, and Japan is GHS-POP. ## Implications for KnoxMap

- The density control that matches the existing footprint model is GHS-POP R2023A. It is worldwide, residential, about 100 m, and licensed CC BY 4.0, which allows a derived count to be copied into a generated mod when the mod credits Schiavina, Freire, Carioli, and MacManus (2023) and the dataset DOI, and indicates that the value was sampled and scaled. The generate step wants one FTP tile for the map’s box and epoch, on the order of megabytes, and can cache that tile. The 4.7 GB global zip is the wrong download for one town. ([copyright.txt](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/copyright.txt), [How to cite](http://ghsl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/GHSLhowToCite.php), [2020 tiles](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/GHS_POP_E2020_GLOBE_R2023A_54009_100/V1-0/tiles/))
- July 1993 is between the 1990 and 1995 GHS epochs. A 1993 factor is an interpolation of those two residential grids, labelled as such. GRUMPv1 also has 1990 and 1995, at about 1 km, and SEDAC directs users to GPWv4, which begins in 2000. UN WPP can supply the 1993 United States total and nothing smaller. ([FTP](https://jeodpp.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ftp/jrc-opendata/GHSL/GHS_POP_GLOBE_R2023A/), [WPP](https://population.un.org/wpp/))
- From 2000, WorldPop’s unconstrained country files are the annual ~100 m series. CC BY 4.0 on the FAQ allows the same kind of attributed reuse as GHS, together with the responsible-use paragraph on that page. The United States file is 3.7 GB per year, so a desktop run either windows that GeoTIFF or uses the stats API and receives one total for the whole polygon. Constrained cells, which leave unsettled land empty, are the 2020 country set in that project, or the 2015–2030 beta. ([FAQ](https://www.worldpop.org/faq/), [USA 2020](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/USA/), [methods](https://www.worldpop.org/methods/top_down_constrained_vs_unconstrained/))
- GPWv4.11 is CC BY 4.0 and is a census surface at about 1 km for 2000–2020, spread evenly inside each administrative unit. It is a check on the national and district total. It is a coarse multiplier inside a town wherever the input unit is large. ([GPWv4 documentation](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/docs/gpw-v4/gpw-v4-documentation-rev11.pdf), [metadata](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/metadata/gpw-v4-population-count-rev11.html))
- LandScan is the ambient, 24-hour surface, annual from 2000 at about 1 km. The data descriptor says the rasters are freely downloadable. It does not state a licence for putting a sampled cell into a mod that users republish, and the portal terms page did not load as text. Until that licence is read, LandScan stays off the baked-in path. ([Scientific Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04817-z))
- HYDE is the layer before 1975, at about 9 km, back to 10 000 BCE. Its year step and its licence were not on the pages that loaded. At that cell size it can only scale a whole map, not a street. ([HYDE datasets](https://landuse.sites.uu.nl/datasets/))
- Eurostat’s 1 km census grid and the 100 m derived grid are Europe, recent census years, and the GISCO page attaches specific download rules that vary by year and country. They are a local check for a European map, after those rules are read. ([GISCO](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/gisco/geodata/population-distribution/population-grids))
- A building-scale historical census for an arbitrary country and year is not a file any of these publishers distribute. Where the map is the United States, NHGIS blocks and tracts are the census record at 1990 and 2000, with 1993 between them. Where the map is England or Wales, Output Areas are the census record at the census date. Those files stay national supplements. They do not replace GHS-POP for the rest of the world. ([NHGIS](https://www.nhgis.org/data-availability), [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/ukgeographies/censusgeographies/census2021geographies))
- Europe, Brazil, the United States, Canada, Mexico, Australia, China, Japan, and Russia are all on GHS-POP R2023A. One tile fetch covers a town in any of them. The census underneath is the 2010 round in GPWv4.11, at administrative level 5 for the United States, Brazil, Australia, and Canada, level 4 for China, level 3 for Mexico, and levels 2 to 3 for Japan and Russia. Inside a coarse unit the 100 m pattern follows residential built-up volume. ([GPWv4 documentation](https://sedac.ciesin.columbia.edu/downloads/docs/gpw-v4/gpw-v4-documentation-rev11.pdf))
- WorldPop’s annual country files exist for the same set, and the large ones are the wrong object for a single map: Russia 16 GB, China 4.6 GB, Brazil 4.1 GB, Australia 3.7 GB, the United States 3.7 GB, Canada 3.6 GB. Japan is 292 MB, Germany 293 MB, the United Kingdom 231 MB. ([RUS](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/RUS/), [CHN](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/CHN/), [JPN](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/JPN/), [DEU](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/DEU/), [GBR](https://data.worldpop.org/GIS/Population/Global_2000_2020/2020/GBR/))
- Eurostat’s 2021 1 km census grid covers the EU plus Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland. The United Kingdom and Russia stay on GHS-POP, as do Brazil, Canada, Mexico, Australia, China, and Japan. ([Census 2021 grids](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Population_and_housing_census_2021_-_population_grids))
