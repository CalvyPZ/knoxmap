# Making OpenStreetMap places recognizable on a tile map

Date: 2026-09-25

Notes from primary sources only: the KnoxMap source and `docs/KNOXBUILD.md`, the OpenStreetMap wiki, the Osmium tool manual, the JOSM help page for Orthogonalize Shape, Esri’s Regularize Building Footprint tool page, the Building Regulariser README, Gribov’s paper abstract as published with the paper, Kim, Lee, and Ahn’s rectangular-partition paper, Boeing’s street-orientation paper, the Pillow, Shapely, GEOS, Clipper, and SciPy manuals, the Overture buildings guide and schema, Microsoft’s Global ML Building Footprints README, the 3DBAG documentation, and the OGC CityGML 3.0 conceptual model. Numbers below are the ones those pages state. A figure that is only in a KnoxMap comment is attributed to that comment.

## 1. What KnoxMap does today

OpenStreetMap marks a building as the outline of a man-made structure with a roof. The tag is for areas, including multipolygon relations. ([Key:building](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:building)) A multipolygon has outer ways for the outline and inner ways for the holes. The wiki’s iD example of a building with a hole says the inner feature can be a courtyard. ([Relation:multipolygon](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:multipolygon))

The compiler does not keep that hole. `renderer._buildings_geojson` writes a way as a Polygon whose only ring is the way, and for a relation it skips every member whose role is `inner`. ([renderer.py](../KnoxMap/generator/renderer.py)) `build._ring_points` then keeps a Polygon’s first ring only, and for a MultiPolygon it concatenates every polygon’s first ring into one point list. ([build.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/build.py)) `footprint._polygon` builds a Shapely `Polygon` from that list. An invalid polygon is replaced with `buffer(0)`, and a MultiPolygon is replaced by its largest piece. ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py))

`docs/KNOXBUILD.md` still describes footprint fitting as one step: `_footprint_rect` takes the minimum rotated rectangle, then places an axis-aligned building of those side lengths, centred on the footprint, because “PZ buildings are axis-aligned grids and cannot be rotated.” The same section records a measurement on 207 Lexington footprints: a plain bounding box inflated area by a median of 2.09 times, and 205 of 207 were oversized by more than 25%. ([KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md))

The function that runs is `footprint.place`, and it is not that step for every building. `grid_angle` measures how far the long side of `minimum_rotated_rectangle` is from the nearest grid axis, in the range 0 to 45 degrees. The polygon is treated as rectangular when its area is at least `RECTANGULAR_ENOUGH` (0.86) of that rotated rectangle. It is replaced by an upright rectangle of the rotated rectangle’s side lengths, centred on the centroid, only when the angle is within `snap_degrees` and either the polygon is rectangular or `snap_degrees` is at least 30. Otherwise each tile is owned when its centre, half a tile in from the corner, lies inside the polygon (`shapely.contains_xy`). ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py))

`Settings.square_buildings` defaults to 15 and is the `snap_degrees` passed into `place`, unless “Knox County roads” is on, in which case the call passes 45. ([settings.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/settings.py), [build.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/build.py)) At 15 degrees a near-rectangular building is squared and anything less rectangular is rasterised, so a diagonal wall becomes a staircase of tile centres. At 45 degrees the `snap_degrees >= 30` branch squares every shape, rectangular or not. The module comment records an earlier measurement, on a Turkish town, that 61% of buildings stood more than 15 degrees off the grid and that 37% of the squared rectangles covered less than 70% of the real footprint. ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py))

`_hug` keeps the largest 4-connected component and trims the box to it. `MIN_TILES` is 16. A footprint is rejected below `min_size` (default 3 tiles on the short side of the rotated rectangle) or above `max_size` (default 200 on the long side). The `.tbx` reader’s dimension cap, as recorded in this repo, is 300, and `max_size` is limited to 250. ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py), [settings.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/settings.py), [KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md))

A placed building can keep a mask. `Plan.mask` is `None` when the building fills its rectangle, and otherwise true only on tiles inside the footprint; tiles outside stay room 0, which BuildingEd treats as not part of the building. ([layout.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/layout.py)) The lot written around that building is still a rectangle. `world.write_project` emits `<lot x y level width height>`. The comment on `_clear_box` says a lot is that rectangle, WorldEd writes the whole rectangle of squares into the cell, and a stair-step footprint therefore claims ground it never occupies. The same comment records 5004 overlapping lot pairs among 4202 buildings on Brugge. A later footprint is cut back to the largest free rectangle inside itself, found by the histogram scan in `_clear_box`. ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py), [world.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/world.py))

Roofs are rectangles too. `roof_rects` covers a storey by repeatedly taking the largest all-true rectangle. The comment says a single bounding-box roof reached over the yard, up to 43% of it over nothing on the buildings measured, and that an L takes two of these rectangles. ([layout.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/layout.py)) Stairs are written only with direction N or W, because the comment says `Stairs::bounds` returns an empty rectangle for anything else and an empty rectangle is an invalid object. ([tbx.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/tbx.py)) Walls sit on the north and west edges of tiles. The reader, as recorded here, accepts versions 1 through 7 and caps dimensions at 300. ([KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md))

The map itself can turn. `dominant_road_angle` averages street bearings with a 90-degree period, weighted by length, over `road_minor`, `road_medium`, and `road_service`. Motorways and paths are excluded. It returns an angle in (−45, 45] degrees and a strength in [0, 1]. `ALIGN_MIN_STRENGTH` is 0.25. The projector then rotates every feature by that angle so the street grid runs along the tiles. The function comment says a street at 20 degrees becomes a staircase. ([renderer.py](../KnoxMap/generator/renderer.py))

With straight roads on, `octilinear.straighten_roads` first drops wiggles under `SIMPLIFY_TILES` (3 tiles) with Douglas–Peucker, then forces each stretch onto one of four directions, the two axes and the two diagonals, and moves the rest of the town by a blurred displacement field (`BLEND_TILES` 30, `FIELD_CELL` 4). Buildings are then placed with `snap_degrees` 45, and `_clear_of` may shift a footprint by up to `NUDGE_TILES` (5) to leave the carriageway. ([octilinear.py](../KnoxMap/generator/octilinear.py), [footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py))

Roads are painted as lines, not as polygons. `_way_width_m` uses `width` or `est_width` when the tag parses, clamped in this file to between 2 and 30, otherwise `lanes` (clamped here to 1 through 8) times 3.2 plus 1.0, otherwise a class width: major 12 m, medium 8 m, minor 6 m, service 3.5 m. `_draw_line` rounds that to pixels and calls Pillow `ImageDraw.line` with `joint="curve"`. Sidewalk and verge are wider lines of the same kind, drawn first. Building footprints are not painted on the landscape bitmap. ([renderer.py](../KnoxMap/generator/renderer.py))

`highway=*` on the wiki distinguishes roads by function and importance, not by physical width. ([Key:highway](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:highway)) `width=*` for a street is the carriageway from kerb to kerb, in metres unless a unit is written, and the wiki says there was no consensus for a long time, so consumers should expect fuzziness. `est_width` is the tag for an estimate. ([Key:width](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:width)) `lanes=*` is a count of motor-traffic lanes, not a width. The wiki says many ways are tagged only with the through lanes of a longer section, so consumers can mostly treat the tag as a minimum, and it tells consumers to tag `width` when lanes are unmarked. Its own assumption table, for when the tag is missing, is 2 lanes for a two-way residential, tertiary, secondary, or primary road, and 1 for unclassified, service, track, and path. ([Key:lanes](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:lanes)) The 3.2 m per lane is this compiler’s formula. It is not on those wiki pages.

With `true_map` off, `procedural.plan` drops ordinary houses that stand closer than `GAP` (1.55) times the distance at which their grown widths would touch, then scales survivors toward `HOUSE_TILES` (90) or a landmark target, with linear caps `GROW_MAX_*` of 1.9 for houses and 2.6 for landmarks. `place` can still cut the grown outline back. ([procedural.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/procedural.py), [KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md))

## 2. Orthogonalization and regularization

Douglas–Peucker, as used in `octilinear._simplify_idx`, deletes points that lie within a distance of a straight chord. It does not force edges onto a direction. ([octilinear.py](../KnoxMap/generator/octilinear.py))

JOSM’s Orthogonalize Shape moves nodes so that every angle of the selected ways is 90 or 180 degrees. The help page says a lot of buildings are not orthogonal in reality, and that orthogonalization does not preserve dimensions or length. Complex buildings are split, orthogonalized in portions, and combined again. Two extra nodes can be selected so that segments align to those nodes. ([Orthogonalize Shape](https://josm.openstreetmap.de/wiki/Help/Action/OrthogonalizeShape))

Esri’s Regularize Building Footprint tool says it uses a polyline compression algorithm to remove artifacts from extracted footprints. The methods it documents are Right Angles (90 degrees between adjoining edges), Right Angles and Diagonals (45 and 90 degrees), Any Angles, and Circle. Tolerance is the maximum distance the regularized footprint may deviate from the original boundary, in the linear units of the input. Densification must be less than or equal to that tolerance. Precision is a spatial-grid value from 0.05 to 0.25. Diagonal penalty 0 means preferred edges are not used and the result is a simplified irregular polygon; a higher penalty makes a diagonal edge less likely. If the tool cannot regularize a feature, it copies the original and sets `STATUS` to 1. Any Angles uses a CUDA GPU when one is present with more than 2 GB of memory. The tool requires a 3D Analyst license. An optional alignment feature is a line; each polygon is aligned to one such line, and the alignment tolerance is the maximum distance at which that line is used. The page’s example of that distance is 20 metres. ([Regularize Building Footprint](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/3d-analyst/regularize-building-footprint.htm))

Gribov’s paper “Optimal Compression of a Polyline While Aligning to Preferred Directions” extends a polyline-compression algorithm so that segments align to a user-defined set of preferred directions. The abstract’s example is building footprints, which it says are usually aligned to the direction of streets and have orthogonal shapes. ([Gribov 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdarw.2019.00022))

Building Regulariser, whose README describes it as an open-source alternative to that Esri tool, aligns edges to a polygon’s principal directions, optionally at 45 degrees, and can turn a near-rectangle into a rectangle and a near-circle into a circle. Documented defaults include `parallel_threshold` 1.0, `simplify_tolerance` 0.5, `allow_45_degree` true, `diagonal_threshold_reduction` 15.0 (allowed range 0 to 22.5), and `circle_threshold` 0.9 as an intersection-over-union. Neighbor alignment, off by default, rotates a building about its centroid toward its neighbours. The documented search distance is 100 and the maximum rotation is 10. ([Building Regulariser](https://github.com/dpird-dma/building-regulariser))

Osmium’s manual documents `osmium extract` and `osmium tags-filter`, including a filter `wr/building` that keeps ways and relations with a building tag. Its `--simplify` flag belongs to `merge-changes` and drops intermediate versions of an object in a change file. The manual does not document a command that changes the angles of a polygon. ([Osmium tool manual](https://osmcode.org/osmium-tool/manual.html))

For a tile compiler the documented constraint is the tolerance, not a free redraw. Esri’s tolerance is a maximum boundary deviation. JOSM’s own page says the 90-degree result does not keep length, and that many real buildings are not orthogonal. A compiler that already squares a building whose long side is within 15 degrees of the grid, and rasterises the rest, is doing a coarser version of the right-angle case: one rectangle, and only when the polygon is already close to a rectangle, unless straight roads forces every footprint through that rectangle.

## 3. Decomposition into rectangles

A rectilinear polygon is one whose edges are already axis-aligned. Kim, Lee, and Ahn study partitioning such a polygon, with no holes, into rectangles by disjoint segments drawn inside it. A minimum-ink partition minimises the total length of those segments, which they show is the same as minimising the total perimeter of the rectangles. They give an algorithm that returns one in O(n³) time and O(n²) space for a polygon of n vertices and no holes. A thick partition maximises the shortest side of any rectangle. With segments required to meet a vertex, they give an algorithm in O(n³ log² n) time and O(n³) space. They state that if the polygon has holes, the decision version of that vertex-incident thick partition is NP-complete. They also state Lingas, Pinter, Rivest, and Shamir’s result that the decision version of minimum-ink partition is strongly NP-complete when the polygon contains holes, and that those authors sketched an O(n⁴) algorithm for the hole-free case. ([Kim, Lee, and Ahn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01970), [Lingas, Pinter, Rivest, and Shamir](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/LPRS82.pdf))

The input those algorithms take is already rectilinear. An OSM ring at an arbitrary angle is not. Section 2 is the step that produces edges at 90 degrees, or at 90 and 45 degrees, inside a stated tolerance. Only the 90-degree result is a rectilinear polygon in the sense of that paper.

`layout.roof_rects` is a different procedure. It repeatedly takes the largest rectangle inside a binary mask and removes it, until the mask is empty. The comment says an L takes two rectangles and a notched block three. It does not claim the minimum ink of Kim, Lee, and Ahn. ([layout.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/layout.py))

A courtyard is an inner ring on the wiki. ([Relation:multipolygon](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:multipolygon)) The partition paper’s NP-completeness results apply to a rectilinear polygon that still has that hole. The hole-free O(n³) algorithm applies after the hole has been dropped, which is what section 1’s export does, or to each piece once the hole has been kept and the wall around it is part of the boundary.

One `.tbx` can already store an L or a notch: room 0 is outside the building, inside the rectangular grid. ([layout.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/layout.py)) The lot in the `.pzw` is still one axis-aligned rectangle of `width` by `height`, and a later lot is cut back so those rectangles do not overlap. ([world.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/world.py), [footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py)) A union of rectangles that is written as several lots is several buildings. A union written as one mask is one lot whose rectangle contains the empty corner.

No primary source opened for this note defines a guillotine partition or a binary space partition as the method for this decomposition. Those names are not used as a result here.

## 4. Aligning buildings to the local street grid

`highway=*` is the key for a road, street, or path, and the value is function and importance. A motorway is a restricted-access divided highway. Residential roads serve housing and do not connect settlements. Service roads are access roads inside an estate, a car park, or an alley. A pedestrian square is a closed way tagged `highway=pedestrian` and `area=yes`. ([Key:highway](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:highway))

Boeing measures street-network orientation from OpenStreetMap. Edges are street segments between intersections, or the raw straight pieces of a way when the graph is not simplified. Each edge contributes a compass bearing and its reciprocal. Bearings go into 36 bins of 10 degrees, shifted by −5 degrees so that 0 and 90 sit at bin centres. Shannon entropy of that histogram is highest, 3.584 nats, when bearings are uniform, and the entropy of an ideal four-way grid is 1.386 nats. Orientation-order φ rescales entropy so that 0 is that uniform distribution and 1 is the single four-way grid. The paper says the most common orientation, even in cities without a strong grid, tends toward north–south–east–west, and that a city can put an organic core next to a later grid. The study’s graphs have over 4.8 million nodes and 3.3 million edges across 100 cities. ([Boeing 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41109-019-0189-1))

`dominant_road_angle` is a length-weighted mean of the same kind of axial direction, with the angle multiplied by 4 before the average so that a 90-degree turn counts as the same axis. It rotates the whole map, buildings included, when the strength is at least 0.25. ([renderer.py](../KnoxMap/generator/renderer.py)) Boeing’s φ is a city-wide score, not a local angle, and the paper’s point that one city contains more than one pattern is the reason a single angle is the wrong turn for a map that contains both an old core and a grid.

Esri’s alignment feature does the local version: each regularized polygon is aligned to one line feature, and only if that line is within the alignment tolerance. ([Regularize Building Footprint](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/3d-analyst/regularize-building-footprint.htm)) Building Regulariser’s neighbor alignment rotates each footprint about its centroid, by at most the documented maximum of 10, toward buildings within the documented search distance of 100. ([Building Regulariser](https://github.com/dpird-dma/building-regulariser)) Gribov’s preferred directions are an input to the compression, and the abstract names the street direction as the reason building edges are orthogonal. ([Gribov 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdarw.2019.00022))

On this compiler the tile axes are fixed. A local street bearing can be used as Esri’s alignment line, or as Gribov’s preferred direction, before `place` rasterises. Rotating one building after that is what `footprint.py` and `KNOXBUILD.md` say Project Zomboid buildings cannot do. Rotating the whole projector is what `dominant_road_angle` already does, for one angle per map.

## 5. Road geometry

The carriageway width is `width=*`, metres by default, kerb to kerb, including on-street cycle lanes and on-street parking lanes, and excluding sidewalks beyond the kerb. The wiki tells consumers to expect fuzziness. An estimate is `est_width`. ([Key:width](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:width)) The lane count is `lanes=*`. It is not a width, it is often only the through lanes, and the page says to tag `width` especially where there are no markings. ([Key:lanes](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:lanes)) Class, in `highway=*`, is importance. ([Key:highway](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:highway)) The compiler’s class widths and the 3.2 m lane formula apply when those tags are absent or fail to parse. ([renderer.py](../KnoxMap/generator/renderer.py))

Pillow draws the wide line. `ImageDraw.line` takes a width in pixels. The only joint it documents is `"curve"`, for rounded edges, or `None`. ([ImageDraw](https://pillow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/ImageDraw.html)) That is the call `_draw_line` makes. Miter and bevel are not parameters of that function.

A vector offset is a different operation. Shapely `offset_curve` returns a line at a signed distance. Outside corners are `round`, `bevel`, or `mitre`. The default join is `round`. The default `mitre_limit` is 5.0, and a mitre point farther than that limit from the vertex is cropped. `quad_segs` defaults to 8 linear segments per quarter circle. ([shapely.offset_curve](https://shapely.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/shapely.offset_curve.html)) GEOS `BufferParameters` defines the same three joins (`JOIN_ROUND`, `JOIN_MITRE`, `JOIN_BEVEL`) and three end caps (`CAP_ROUND`, `CAP_FLAT`, `CAP_SQUARE`). The default quadrant segments are 8, which the manual says gives less than 2% maximum error in the buffer distance; 12 is the figure it gives for under 1%, and 18 for under 0.1%. The computed round curve is always inside the true arc. A mitre that exceeds the mitre-ratio limit is beveled. ([BufferParameters](https://libgeos.org/doxygen/classgeos_1_1operation_1_1buffer_1_1BufferParameters.html)) Clipper’s `JoinType` is `Miter`, `Square`, `Bevel`, or `Round`, used when offsetting. Concave joins are always mitered. A miter is squared off when the distance from the original vertex exceeds the miter limit, whose default the page states as 2, expressed as a ratio of the offset distance. A bevel is the cheaper truncation; a square join puts the midpoint of the truncating edge exactly one offset away from the original vertex. ([JoinType](https://www.angusj.com/clipper2/Docs/Units/Clipper/Types/JoinType.htm))

Those joins are properties of the vector outline. They are not a statement, in those manuals, about how a tile rasteriser will step a diagonal edge. What they change is the corner where two carriageway edges meet: a round join approximates an arc with the documented quadrant segments, a bevel cuts the corner back to the vertex, and a mitre extends the edges until they meet, then gives up and bevels if the spike exceeds the limit.

On the raster, SciPy `binary_closing` is an erosion of a dilation. The manual says closing fills holes smaller than the structuring element, and that it can coarsen a boundary that has fine hollows. The default structuring element connects nearest neighbours and does not treat diagonal neighbours as connected. The example of a 2×2 element fills a one-pixel notch that the default element leaves open. ([binary_closing](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.ndimage.binary_closing.html)) `_draw_line` does not call it. The manual’s first example is a one-pixel interior hole, which the default element fills. Its second example is a one-pixel notch in the boundary, which the default element leaves and a 2×2 element fills. The manuals do not say that closing removes a staircase produced by rasterising a diagonal.

`octilinear` is the compiler’s own substitute for a jagged centreline: Douglas–Peucker at 3 tiles, then a least-squares move onto the four directions the module comment says the game draws well. ([octilinear.py](../KnoxMap/generator/octilinear.py))

## 6. Richer source data

Overture’s building geometry is the outer footprint, or the roofprint when it was traced from imagery. `height` is metres from the lowest point to the highest, and must be greater than 0 when present. `num_floors` is the count of above-ground floors, greater than 0 when present. `class` is a use such as `house`, `apartments`, `school`, or `hospital`. `has_parts` says there are `building_part` features. A part has its own footprint and can carry `min_height` and `min_floor` so that a part which bridges a courtyard entrance is not extruded from the ground. The schema does not define interior rooms. ([Building](https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/buildings/building/), [BuildingPart](https://docs.overturemaps.org/schema/reference/buildings/building_part/))

The buildings guide says geometry is kept identical to the source. OpenStreetMap has the highest conflation priority. `building_part` comes only from OpenStreetMap. Machine-learning footprints must have an area greater than 10 m to enter conflation. A feature whose height is 900 m or more is excluded. A match requires intersection-over-union above 0.5. The theme is global and monthly. Many footprints in the Global South are machine-learning roofprints of lower precision. Polygons with too many sharp angles are excluded. The guide’s use cases for height and floor count are 2D display and extrusion to 3D or 2.5D. Places of business are excluded from this theme because they belong to another theme. ([Buildings guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/buildings/))

Microsoft’s README says Bing Maps detected 1.4 billion buildings from imagery between 2014 and 2024, including Maxar, Airbus, and IGN France, under CDLA Permissive 2.0. The same file’s FAQ still describes the download as 999 million footprint polygons in line-delimited GeoJSON, stored as `.csv.gz`. The extraction steps it documents are semantic segmentation of building pixels, then polygonization. Height is the average, in metres, of a neural-network estimate inside the polygon. Structures without a height are stored as −1. Confidence is an average of per-pixel probabilities and applies to the footprint, not the height. The README does not describe interior rooms, floor counts, or part decomposition. A refresh note dated 2026-08-13 says tile coverage was 30,340 tiles across 225 regions. ([Global ML Building Footprints](https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints))

3DBAG is a Netherlands-wide set of 3D building models generated from BAG building data and AHN elevation. It publishes LoD1.2, LoD1.3, and LoD2.2. LoD1.2 is one height for the whole model. LoD1.3 distinguishes significant height differences inside one building. LoD2 adds semantic surfaces, and LoD2.2 models the roof planes found in the point cloud. The 2D layers are the projection of those roof surfaces. LoD1.2 and LoD1.3 can be rebuilt by extruding the 2D polygons between ground height and a roof-height percentile. The documentation says the slanted LoD2.2 planes cannot be rebuilt from the 2D polygons and a single height. The set contains buildings, not the rest of the city model. ([3DBAG overview](https://docs.3dbag.nl/en/), [Concepts](https://docs.3dbag.nl/en/schema/concepts/), [Data layers](https://docs.3dbag.nl/en/schema/layers/))

OSM Simple 3D Buildings describes volume with two kinds of area: `building=*` for the outline, which is the land covered by the union of the parts, and `building:part=*` for sections that differ in height or other attributes. Attributes such as name and overall height go on the outline. `height` is the distance from the lowest ground contact to the top of the roof, excluding antennas and spires. `building:levels` is the number of floors above ground, without the floors in the roof. The page says the scheme is limited and cannot describe every detail. `roof:shape` values it catalogues include flat, skillion, gabled, half-hipped, hipped, pyramidal, gambrel, mansard, dome, onion, round, and saltbox. 2D renderers ignore the part attributes. When any part exists, the outline is not used for 3D rendering. ([Simple 3D Buildings](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_3D_Buildings))

CityGML 3.0 defines four levels of detail for both interior and exterior. LOD0 is a highly generalized model. LOD1 is a block model of extrusion objects. LOD2 is a realistic but still generalized model. LOD3 is a highly detailed model. LOD1 spaces are vertical extrusions of a horizontal footprint. LOD0 can be a footprint or a floor plan of the rooms. Buildings can be subdivided into `BuildingPart`s. A building can contain `BuildingRoom`s. The standard says all of its levels of detail allow an interior, and that an exterior shell in LOD2 can sit beside interior rooms, doors, hallways, and stairs in LOD1. ([CityGML 3.0 conceptual model](https://docs.ogc.org/is/20-010/20-010.html))

What each source adds, in the terms those pages use, is the outer polygon, and in some cases a height, a floor count, a part with its own height, or a roof shape. Overture, Microsoft, 3DBAG, and Simple 3D Buildings do not, on the pages above, provide the rooms inside. CityGML’s model allows those rooms. None of that interior, where it exists, is a Project Zomboid furniture layout; the `.tbx` this repo writes is walls, floors, and furniture, and container contents are not in the file. ([KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md))

## 7. Recognizable landmarks without full geometry

`name=*` is the primary name of a feature, the one a data consumer is expected to show as a label. It is the name only, not a description of the object. A street that has no name can be tagged `noname=yes` so the absence is deliberate. A way can carry `name:left` and `name:right` when the two sides have different names. ([Key:name](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:name))

`landuse=*` is the primary human use of an area: residential, commercial, retail, industrial, military, cemetery, and others. The page says a more specific use is often carried by `amenity`, `leisure`, or `tourism` instead of, or as well as, `landuse`. ([Key:landuse](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:landuse)) `leisure=park` is an open green area for recreation, usually municipal. `leisure=pitch` is a sports field. `leisure=playground` is a playground. ([Key:leisure](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:leisure)) `natural=water` is any body of water, from a lake or pond to a moat or canal. `natural=wood` is a tree-covered area. `natural=coastline` is the mean high-water line, with the water on the right of the way. ([Key:natural](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:natural))

`bridge=*` on a way says that way is on a bridge. The mapping instruction is to split the upper way where it meets solid ground and tag the middle `bridge=yes`. The outline of a large bridge is a separate object, `man_made=bridge`. The name of the bridge and the name of the road on it are different tags when both exist: `name` stays the signposted road name, and `bridge:name` or the outline carries the bridge name. ([Key:bridge](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:bridge)) `railway=rail` is full-size passenger or freight track. `railway=station` is a passenger and/or cargo station. A level crossing is `railway=level_crossing`. ([Key:railway](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:railway))

This compiler already paints those areas, in an order that puts land use down and roads on top, and it does not paint building footprints on the ground bitmap. Water, parks, residential land, railway beds, and bridges are categories in that paint order. Named places with a `population` tag are written as points. Building names are among the tags copied onto the building GeoJSON. ([renderer.py](../KnoxMap/generator/renderer.py)) The paper map labels a limited set of named buildings (`MAX_BUILDING_LABELS` is 40) and draws highway, railway, and area polygons from the same features. ([worldmap.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/worldmap.py))

The street name graph is the `name` on the `highway` way. Simplifying a building polygon does not remove that tag. Dropping the way, or painting every class at one width, removes the distinction the wiki encodes as function (`highway`) and as a separate carriageway measure (`width`, `lanes`).

## 8. What has to survive

The objects the sources define, and that a simplification can delete, are these.

The building is the outline, and for a complex building the outline is the union of the parts. A hole is an inner ring, and the wiki’s own building example of that ring is a courtyard. ([Simple 3D Buildings](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_3D_Buildings), [Relation:multipolygon](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Relation:multipolygon)) Section 1 drops the inner ring before `place` runs, so the courtyard is not available to the mask.

The street pattern Boeing measures is the set of bearings of the street segments. φ is how close those bearings are to one four-way grid. A city can contain more than one such pattern side by side. ([Boeing 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41109-019-0189-1)) One projector rotation is one angle. It straightens the streets that share that angle and turns the streets that do not.

Open space is an area tag, not a gap left after buildings are squared. `landuse`, `leisure=park`, and `natural=water` are those areas. ([Key:landuse](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:landuse), [Key:leisure](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:leisure), [Key:natural](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:natural)) A minimum-rotated-rectangle building that is larger than the outline occupies ground those areas, or the neighbouring lot, had. The Brugge comment records that overlap as lots, not as shared walls. ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py))

What the regularization sources say can change is the edge direction, inside a bound. Esri’s tolerance is the maximum distance the new boundary may leave the old one. JOSM moves nodes to 90 and 180 degrees and states that length is not preserved, and that many buildings are not orthogonal. Building Regulariser snaps edges to the principal direction, or to 45 degrees when that option is on, and can rotate a footprint about its centroid by at most its maximum rotation. ([Regularize Building Footprint](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/tool-reference/3d-analyst/regularize-building-footprint.htm), [Orthogonalize Shape](https://josm.openstreetmap.de/wiki/Help/Action/OrthogonalizeShape), [Building Regulariser](https://github.com/dpird-dma/building-regulariser))

What this repo says cannot change, once a building is written, is the orientation of that building relative to the tile axes. The lot is an axis-aligned rectangle. The room grid may leave cells empty. Stairs are N or W. A roof piece is a rectangle. The reader cap recorded here is 300 on a side. The map’s cell is 300 tiles. `max_levels` is limited to the range 1 through 30; the same file says Build 42 has 32 levels above ground and that WorldEd’s `MAX_WORLD_LEVELS` is 64, from −32 to 31. ([footprint.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/footprint.py), [world.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/world.py), [tbx.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/tbx.py), [layout.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/layout.py), [settings.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/settings.py), [KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md))

With `true_map` off, thinning and growing change which houses exist and how large they are. That is a separate change from regularization. The documented targets are 90 tiles for a house and larger fixed targets for landmarks, with the linear caps above. ([KNOXBUILD.md](KNOXBUILD.md), [procedural.py](../KnoxMap/knoxbuild/procedural.py))

## 9. Comparison

| Approach | Visual failure it addresses | What the cited source keeps | Cost the source states |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current `place`: square when the long side is within `square_buildings` and the polygon is rectangular enough, otherwise tile-centre raster | Diagonal walls become steps; near-axis rectangles become one box | Side lengths of the minimum rotated rectangle in the squared case; tile centres inside the ring in the other case. Inner rings are already gone. | One `.tbx` per footprint. Lot rectangle can cover tiles the mask does not. Reader cap 300. Default skip outside 3 and 200 tiles. |
| Whole-map `dominant_road_angle` | Streets that share one axis stop being staircases | Every feature rotates together. Streets off that axis do not straighten. Applied only when strength ≥ 0.25. | One angle per map, in (−45, 45]. |
| `octilinear` straight roads | Centreline not on an axis or a diagonal | Junctions stay shared. Buildings move with the displacement field, then `place` is called at 45 degrees, which squares every footprint. | Douglas–Peucker at 3 tiles, then up to 400 conjugate-gradient iterations. Nudge of at most 5 tiles. |
| JOSM orthogonalize, or Esri Right Angles, before rasterizing | Stair-step and bent extracted edges become 90-degree edges | Esri: boundary stays within the tolerance. JOSM: angles, not lengths. Not every real building is orthogonal. | Esri copies the original when it fails. Any Angles can use a CUDA GPU with more than 2 GB. 3D Analyst license. |
| Esri Right Angles and Diagonals, or Building Regulariser with 45 degrees | Same, while keeping a diagonal wing | 45-degree edges as well as 90. Higher diagonal penalty makes diagonals less likely. | Same Esri tolerance and precision grid (0.05 to 0.25). Regulariser: per-polygon work, optional use of all CPU cores. |
| Esri alignment line, or a local street bearing as a preferred direction | A diagonal block stays one orientation instead of a mix of steps and north-up boxes | Each polygon aligns to one line within the alignment tolerance. Boeing: a city may contain more than one grid. | One extra line query per polygon. Does not rotate the `.tbx`; the result still has to lie on the tile axes or be rasterised. |
| Rectangle partition of a hole-free rectilinear polygon | An L, U, or step is several rectangles instead of one bounding box | The union of the rectangles is the polygon. Minimum ink is the minimum total cut length. | O(n³) time and O(n²) space in Kim, Lee, and Ahn. With holes, they state that minimum-ink decision is strongly NP-complete and that vertex-incident thick partition is NP-complete. |
| One `.tbx` mask of room 0, which this repo already writes | The empty corner of an L is not a room | The lot is still the bounding rectangle, and the next lot is cut back so the rectangles do not overlap. | One file. Roof pieces are further rectangles, largest first. |
| Buffered centreline (GEOS, Shapely, Clipper) instead of Pillow `joint="curve"` | Corner shape: round, bevel, or mitre, with a mitre limit | Carriageway width still has to come from `width`, or from a count in `lanes` plus a width this compiler invents. | Shapely default mitre limit 5.0 and 8 segments per quarter circle. GEOS says 8 segments give less than 2% maximum arc error. Clipper’s default miter limit is 2. |
| `binary_closing` on the painted road | Interior holes smaller than the structuring element. A larger element also fills fine boundary hollows the default leaves. | The manual’s default element does not connect diagonal neighbours. It does not say this removes a raster staircase. | One dilation and one erosion per iteration. Not used by `_draw_line`. |
| Overture building, or Microsoft polygons, where OSM has no outline | Missing footprints. Overture also has height, `num_floors`, `class`, and OSM parts. Microsoft has a height on some polygons, else −1. | Overture keeps source geometry, OSM first. Microsoft polygons come from image pixels. Neither schema here includes rooms. | Overture: monthly global theme, ML footprints only above 10 m area, heights of 900 m and above dropped. Microsoft README: 1.4 billion detections; FAQ text still says 999 million polygons. |
| 3DBAG or CityGML LOD1 | Height and, at LoD1.3 or with parts, a building that is not one height | 3DBAG LoD1 is an extrusion of the roof projection. CityGML LOD1 is an extrusion of a horizontal footprint. CityGML also allows rooms; 3DBAG’s pages describe the exterior models. | 3DBAG is the Netherlands, from BAG and AHN. CityGML is a model, not a world file. |
| Keep `name`, `landuse`, `leisure=park`, `natural=water`, `bridge`, `railway`, and the highway way | A simplified building mass still sits in a named street, a park, a river, or beside a station | Those tags are on the area or the way, not on the regularity of the footprint. | Paint and labels this compiler already writes. `MAX_BUILDING_LABELS` is 40. |
